[mindfork 0.28.0](https://github.com/vshylov/mindfork-rs/releases) is out. A
conversation can now go back to any of its messages and go another way, and nothing is
lost: what followed is kept as a branch of the same chat. Every message shows its
number, which is how you name the place to go back to. And the conversation stays where
you are reading: a resize, a reply arriving in the background, or a reference followed
and come back from no longer moves you.

## Going back to a message

`/fork N` goes back to message N. What followed it is kept as a branch of the same
chat. Nothing is copied into a new chat, and nothing is deleted.
- **At a reply of the assistant**, the conversation continues after it: your next
  message goes there.
- **At your own message**, its text returns to the message box, so you can write it
  again. This is the way to change a message in the middle of a conversation.
- **`/fork` alone** lists the messages to pick from.

A note under the conversation names the command that returns to what you left.

## Switching between branches

A message that has other variants says which one it is, before its number:
`‹2/3› #11` is the second of three ways the conversation went from there.
- `/branch N K` switches message N to variant K, and `/branch` alone lists every branch
  of the chat.
- With the mouse, a click on `‹` or `›` goes to the neighbouring variant. The message
  you switch stays where it is on the screen, so you can click back and forth to
  compare.
- Coming back to a branch restores the conversation as you left it.

With `/fork` or `/branch` in the box, a click on a message's number writes it in.
Nothing runs until `Enter`.

## `Ctrl+R` and `Ctrl+E` keep what they replace

**`Ctrl+R`.** A regenerated reply is a new variant of the same message, `‹2/2›` on its
header, and `‹` brings the previous one back. Before, the old reply went into an
archive in the chat's file that nothing in the app could show or return.

**`Ctrl+E`.** The exchange it takes back stays in the chat as a branch. Your text still
returns to the box, and the note under the conversation names the command that returns
to the exchange.

The setting that asks before these two keys is now *Confirm regenerate / take back*. It
is still off by default, and its questions say what is kept.

## What the assistant sees

The assistant reads the conversation in front of you. A branch you left is not in its
request and not in what its tools find. A fork changes the conversation and nothing
else: notes the assistant saved, files it wrote and changes it made to your project stay
as they are.

**When you want it to look at a branch**, two new tools let it. Both are **off by
default**, switches among the profile's tools (Settings → Profiles → *Conversation
control*), because a branch is also a reply you regenerated or an exchange you took
back.
- `branch_read` lists a chat's branches and reads one: "compare this with what you
  answered before I regenerated". With it on, the assistant is also told when a
  conversation it reads has branches; with it off, it is told nothing about them.
- `branch_search` finds the branch that says a word: "which earlier variant mentioned
  the harbour master?" It searches the branches of one conversation, never the
  conversation in front of you.

Self-reflection reads the same signals from the branches. A regenerated reply or an
exchange taken back still tells it a reply did not land; a reply you went back to no
longer counts as one.

## Your search

A new setting, **Search in parked branches** (Settings → Interface → Behavior), is off by
default. With it on, the search by content also finds messages in a chat's branches:
the chat list's filter, `Ctrl+G` and `/search`.
- Such a match is shown under its chat as a branch, named as `/branch` names it.
- `Enter` opens the chat where that branch leaves the conversation and says which branch
  holds the match. Nothing is switched.

`mindfork stats` counts the messages kept in branches on a line of its own, and
`--compare` tells a fork from a loss: a copy missing a branch is behind by what the
branch held.

## Messages are numbered

Every message shows its number, `#12`, at the right end of its header. The last one is
the count the chat list shows for the chat, and `/copy last N` counts the same messages.
The number is not part of the text: a selection does not take it and a copy does not
hold it.

## The conversation stays where you are reading

**What arrives by itself no longer moves you.** A background run's result, a follow-up,
a message from the messenger, and the reply the assistant starts on one of them used to:
- scroll the conversation to its end, wherever you were reading;
- close the search in it and the open list;
- reset the message box, so the cursor jumped and `Ctrl+Z` forgot what you had typed.

Now the conversation moves only if you are at its end, and nothing else is touched.

**A resize keeps your place.** Scrolled into the middle of a conversation, a resize used
to leave you on some other message, and so did `Ctrl+T`, `Ctrl+O` or a theme. The line
at the top of the window now stays there, through any number of resizes.

**Where a conversation opens:**
- a `chat://` reference opens it at its beginning, and `Esc` returns to the place you
  followed it from;
- a search result opens with its match on the screen, not at the first line of a long
  message;
- a finished sub-agent transcript, and a greeting swapped with `/greeting`, open at
  their first line.

## Your data

The chats move to schema 9. The step changes nothing in a file but its version, and
the first start backs every chat up first. The stamp keeps 0.27.0 from opening a chat
with branches and dropping them at its first save: it refuses the data as a newer
version's instead.

**Going back to 0.27.0 means restoring that backup** (`backups/pre-migrate-….zip`) with
0.27.0. This was checked with the published 0.27.0:
- on data this version wrote, it stops at start, names a chat file, and changes nothing;
- it restores the backup with no warning and starts on it. What you wrote since,
  branches included, is not in it.

Replies regenerated and exchanges taken back before this version stay where they were,
in the chat file's archive; they do not become branches. The search index is rebuilt
once at the first start, by itself.
