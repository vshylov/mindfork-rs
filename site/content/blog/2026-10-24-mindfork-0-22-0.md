[mindfork 0.22.0](https://github.com/vshylov/mindfork-rs/releases) is out. It makes a
profile something you can keep talking to over weeks: it knows when you last spoke,
remembers what you told it from the first message, comes back to you on its own, and
answers from your phone. Every part of it is a switch of the profile, off by default:
an assistant profile, and a fresh install, work as before.

## It knows when, and what you told it

- **The time of messages.** With *Time of messages* on, each of your messages reaches
  the model with when it was written and how long after the one before — "[Thursday,
  22 October 2026, 18:40 · 2d 21h after the previous message]" — so it knows that three
  days have passed since you said the interview was tomorrow. The rows are worked out
  when the request is built; nothing is added to your chats, and the prompt cache holds.
- **Your notes, from the first message.** *Notes in the prompt* puts the notes the
  assistant wrote about you that are close to how a conversation opens into its
  prompt. Measured on five models: Gemma 4, which never searched its notes on its
  own, used the fact it needed 13 times in 15 with the block, from once without. A
  "latest notes" fallback was measured too, and left out: it carried your freshest
  news into answers about TCP and the planets on every model.
- **Earlier conversations.** *Earlier conversations in the prompt* lists the three
  before this one, so "let's pick up where we left off" works.
- **`/notes`** shows every note the assistant keeps about you — search it, read where a
  note came from and what it replaced, edit it, delete it — and which of them this
  conversation's prompt carries.

## It comes back, and tells you

- **Follow-ups.** The assistant can plan to come back to something you told it — how
  the interview went, the reminder you asked for on Friday at 9 — and writes first when
  the time comes. It plans only on what you said, may decide there is nothing to say
  and then says nothing; quiet hours (22 to 8) and a cap of three a day are on from the
  start. Every planned follow-up is on the tasks screen (`F7`), where `Del` twice
  cancels it.
- **Notifications.** *Notify me* tells you when the companion writes while you are not
  looking: a desktop notification through the terminal (OSC 777 or OSC 9 — Ghostty,
  WezTerm, kitty, foot, iTerm2, Windows Terminal Preview 1.26 with
  `compatibility.allowOSC777`, tmux with `allow-passthrough all`), the bell, and a push
  to your phone through ntfy if you give a topic. Only who wrote and where is sent,
  unless you ask for the words.

## An instrument for watching a self

- **The self-model's history.** Every change to the self-model is kept with who made
  it — which model, in which conversation, or a background reflection, or you. `H` on
  `F3` lists the versions with what each changed; `mindfork stats --self-model` prints
  the whole timeline as JSON, from your data or from a backup.
- **Fork a companion.** `/profile fork` copies a profile with its whole memory — notes,
  knowledge base, self-model and its history — without its chats. Give the fork another
  model and watch the two selves diverge; the history marks where the fork began.
- **Carry one.** `mindfork companion export` writes one companion into an archive,
  encrypted if you give a password, and `mindfork companion import` adds it on another
  machine as a new profile.

## Characters, and a voice

- **Character cards.** `mindfork import` reads the community's cards — `.json`, `.png`
  with the card inside, `.charx` — into a new profile, shows what will become the
  system message before it asks, and runs nothing from a card. Example dialogue goes
  in as examples the character speaks, with the other side a nameless visitor: with
  your name there, one model of five took the examples for things you had told it.
  {% raw %}`{{char}}`, `{{user}}` and `{{random:…}}`{% endraw %} work in any persona; `/greeting` moves to a
  card's next opening.
- **A voice of its own.** A profile can have its own voice under each speech
  provider, and *Speak replies* reads each reply aloud as it arrives.

## From your phone

*Messenger bridge* makes a profile a Telegram bot of its own while the app runs: make
the bot with @BotFather, paste its token, type `/bridge pair` and open the link on your
phone. Your messages get a conversation of their own, answered while the terminal does
something else; *typing…*, then the whole reply; `/new` and `/stop` from the phone. A
follow-up the companion planned there reaches your phone.

- **Pictures** — a photo, an image file, an album — reach the companion with their
  captions.
- **Voice messages** are heard by the speech recognition you pick under
  *Model/server → Recognition*: OpenAI (`gpt-transcribe`), any of OpenRouter's
  recognisers, or a server of your own such as Whisper on vLLM. Sixteen phone-shaped
  notes on fifteen recognisers chose that design: every transcription API took a
  Telegram note as it is, and no single model was best at Russian. The conversation
  keeps the words, marked as spoken, not the recording, and the model is told it reads
  a transcript — tried on a real phone in very noisy surroundings, it flagged a word the recogniser had
  dropped. With *Speak replies* on, a voice message is answered with one, after the
  text. Voice notes are read and written by the app itself, in Rust; nothing to
  install.
- **What a reply from the phone cannot do**: run code, write files, edit a project or
  call an MCP server. Only your account is answered.
- **Who can read it.** A chat with a bot is not end-to-end encrypted: Telegram can read
  it, and a voice message is heard by the recogniser you picked. The settings say so
  before the switch is on, and the [privacy policy](/privacy/) has the details.

## Your data

No schema step: everything new is a field, a section or a table that an older version
skips. Checked with 0.21.0 on a copy of data this version wrote: it reads all of it.
The one thing it loses is the two new sections of `settings.json` — notifications and
speech recognition — which it drops when it saves its settings, at start; a profile's
companion settings are dropped only if 0.21.0 saves the profiles. Notes and backups do
not change.

Four security advisories published after 0.21.0 no longer apply: the bundled root
certificates and the certificate verifier refuse the distrusted emSign intermediates,
and `tokio` and `ctutils` are past two more.
