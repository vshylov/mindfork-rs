[mindfork 0.24.0](https://github.com/vshylov/mindfork-rs/releases) is out. The
assistant can now write a story with you, a chapter at a time, on whatever model you
chat with. It is experimental and off by default: a chapter takes some fifty calls to
the model and can take an hour or more.

## A story from your sketches

- **Turn it on** in Settings → Tools → *Stories (experimental)*, attach your notes on a
  story — `/file attach notes.md` — and ask for a story of them.
- **The canon first.** The assistant composes the world's rules, the places and the
  ways between them, the characters with their goals and secrets, the style and the
  chapters, and shows you the premise, the cast and the plan. A canon with a problem is
  refused with each one named — a place no way leads to is one — and fixed before you
  see it.
- **Your answer is your own words.** Nothing is written until you approve, and when
  the assistant says you did, the app reads the messages you wrote since the
  question, never the assistant's summary of them. "Yes, go ahead" approves; "maybe?"
  or a question back does not, and the assistant is told to ask you.

## A chapter written, read and written again

Each chapter is written in the background (`F7` lists it) by one writer, the chat's
own model. It drafts the chapter, reads it as its editor would — a conflict that goes
slack, a secret spent too soon, a name used two ways, facts that disagree with an
earlier chapter — and writes it again, with the chapters you accepted in front of it.
The chapter lands in the chat as an attachment, with its question: accept it, with
wishes for the next one if you like, or send it back with your notes.

A chapter is bounded by a time limit (6 hours) and a budget (6,000,000 tokens, or a
dollar cap where the provider reports cost); on Claude Haiku 5.5 one took about 3.3
million tokens. A run that stops says where, and resumes there only when you ask.
`story_status` tells the assistant where each story of the chat stands, so "what is
my story doing?" gets an answer, not a guess.

The writer was chosen by a blind judging: three models preferred its stories to
those of the engine the tools were first built on, where characters acted each scene
out under a world that ruled on it. So that engine is not in the tools.

## The line-break key, chosen

*Settings → Interface → Line break key* picks the key that breaks a line in the input
box: `auto`, as before, or `Shift+Enter`, `Alt+Enter`, `Ctrl+Enter` or `Ctrl+J`. Over
SSH from Windows Terminal the app used to name `Alt+Enter`, which Windows Terminal
keeps for full screen; `Ctrl+Enter` and `Ctrl+J` work there, and `Shift+Enter`, which
arrives there as a plain `Enter`, is marked as such in the setting.

## Fixed

- **Thinking off reaches Claude.** With thinking turned off, Claude Haiku 5.5, Sonnet
  5.5, Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 still thought before every answer. A turn with thinking
  off now asks each model for it off in the shape it takes; Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1,
  which always think, think at their lowest effort.
- **A restore no longer writes over the copy it is restoring.** Restoring the copy
  `restore` had just saved, within the same second, gave the new copy the same name,
  and the data the copy kept was lost. An archive the app names itself now never takes
  a name that exists.
- **A file in `chats/` that holds no chat no longer stops the start.** A JSON file
  there that is not a chat — `{}`, a list, a note saved into the folder — refused every
  start. It is now skipped with a line in the log, and left as it is.

## Your data

The chat files move to schema 8, because a chat may now hold the record of a story's
run, and 0.23.1 would have left such a chat out of its list without a word. Checked
with the published 0.23.1 on data this version wrote: it stops at start, says the data
comes from a newer version, and changes nothing. A backup is made before the step;
restored with 0.23.1, it comes back with no warning and every chat in it. Backups now
carry your stories. The new line-break setting takes no step: an older version drops
it when it saves its settings.
