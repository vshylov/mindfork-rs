[mindfork 0.26.4](https://github.com/vshylov/mindfork-rs/releases) is out. It changes
where the companion keeps what it learns about you, and fixes three things in its
memory and search tools.

## What it learns about you goes into its notes

A companion with the self-model tools on keeps two kinds of memory. Notes hold what it
knows about you and your work. Observations hold what it has understood about itself.
Note search looks at the notes; the observations are the companion's own history.

Facts about you mostly ended up among the observations: a move, your job, a plan, a
view you hold. There note search did not find them by default, and they crowded out
what the companion had learned about itself. Its rules now ask one question first:
would this still be true if the companion did not exist? A fact about you would, so it
goes into a tagged note. What the companion understood about itself stays an
observation.

This was measured on two models over the same scripted conversations. The facts kept
out of the observations went from 2 of 18 on Gemma 4 31B and 13 of 18 on Gemini to 18
of 18 on both.

Facts already filed as observations stay where they are. When a note is saved, any
observation that holds the same fact is now listed beside the similar notes, and
merging the two gives a note. Before, such a merge turned the note into an
observation. A merge of observations is still an observation.

A note is now written as a statement that names who it is about — "Anna moved to
Porto", not "Moved to Porto". It still makes sense when read months later, and a fact
said again is recognised as the same one.

## "A possible duplicate" means one

`note_save` and `add_insight` showed the three nearest notes after every write, under
a hint to merge them, however far those notes were. In one conversation, all nine such
items were on a merely neighbouring topic.

Now a note is shown only when it is close enough to be a repeat, or a conflicting
version of the same fact. A real duplicate is no longer pushed out of the list by notes
of the other kind.

## A search with one word found nowhere

Searching other conversations or the folded-away part of this one needs every word in
one message. So a single word that appeared nowhere emptied the whole result. That
word is now left out and named, and the rest is searched in the same call. When every
word occurs but never in one message, the answer says how many messages hold each one.

Two-letter words such as `AI` and `C#` are searched too, as whole words. They used to
be ignored, so a query made only of them was refused.

## Your data

Nothing is migrated: the settings stay at schema 7 and the chats at 8. Going back to
0.26.3 needs no backup restored. This was checked with 0.26.3 on notes this version
wrote, among them an observation merged into a note.
