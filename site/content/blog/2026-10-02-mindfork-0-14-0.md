[mindfork 0.14.0](https://github.com/vshylov/mindfork-rs/releases) is out. Two
pieces of work are in it, and neither adds a screen: the app in a small window,
and the reasoning effort on models that each take a different part of the scale.

## A small window

A terminal window can be dragged down to a few rows, and split panes get there
without anyone meaning to. Until now the app drew what fitted and left the rest
out: at 57×5 the input box lost its row of text and its prompt landed on the
status bar; a tool confirmation's keys — the ones that answer it — were cut off
at the corner, its legend being 69 columns wide in Russian.

Two rules replace that.

**The chat gives up its chrome, one piece at a time.** In a window of eleven
rows or more nothing changes. Below that the status bar keeps one row, then the
input box loses its border, then the feed does, then the status row goes — so a
window of five rows is four rows of the conversation over the `❯` prompt, and
three rows are still a chat. The key hints a border carried are on `F1`.

**A window too small for what is in front says so.** Below what a screen needs
— 20×3 for the chat, 46×12 for the settings — the app shows one line: *Window
too small*, the size the window is and the size it needs. While it is up the
keys do nothing but quit, so `Enter` cannot answer a question that is not on the
screen; `Esc` also works when what does not fit was opened over the chat — a
picker, the help, the settings — and closes it.

The same pass went over everything a narrow window used to cut:

- A screen's key legend takes a third of the window at most. In Russian the
  chat list's legend wrapped to 13 rows of a 14-row window, leaving one chat
  above it; past a third it now leaves hints out, keeping `F1` and `Esc` first.
- The status bar shows every indicator whole that fits and leaves out the rest —
  it used to be cut mid-number, and what fell off during a turn was the token
  counter.
- A box's title drops whole hints rather than ending mid-word; two titles on one
  border no longer run into each other; the help's tabs stay whole, with `…`
  where others are hidden; and the chat's title is no longer squeezed out by the
  model's name.
- No cursor blinks through a tool confirmation or through the help.

At 80×24 nothing looks different.

## One reasoning effort, every model

The reasoning effort is one setting, and no two models take the same part of
its scale. Measured across ten OpenAI models, there are five different sets:
nine of the ten refuse `minimal`, five take `max`, and `gpt-6.1-sol` cannot
switch reasoning off where `gpt-6-sol` can. The provider's answer to a value a
model lacks is an error — so an effort chosen for one model made every message
to the next one fail.

**A value the model does not have is now the nearest it has.** The request goes
out as chosen; if the model refuses it and says what it takes, the app asks
once more with the closest value on the list — the lower one on a tie, never
more reasoning than you asked for where less is as near — and remembers the
answer for that model.

**The scale has a new top: `max`**, after `xhigh` — the tier the newest OpenAI
and Claude models have. On a model or a provider without it the nearest tier is
used: `xhigh` on Grok and on the older GPT models, `high` on Gemini. And
`xhigh` on Claude is now `xhigh`: it had been sent as `high` since before the
API had the tier, so a chat set to it on Opus 4.7 or newer reasons deeper than
it did, at that tier's price.

**Grok's efforts are read from xAI's own list.** A chat's title, the history
summary, a message written on your behalf and a page's retelling ask the model
not to reason. On Grok that request was dropped, and each of them reasoned at
the model's default depth. The app now reads what xAI says each model takes:
Grok 4.3 is switched off for those requests — measured, 0.7–0.8 seconds where
they took three or four — and the newer models, which cannot be switched off,
are asked for `low` instead of their default `high`. The thinking switch, which
did nothing on Grok, now works there.

## Models that answer again

The same work turned up models on which the app's own defaults were an error
on every message:

- **Claude Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 4.5 and Opus 4.5.** Thinking is on by default, and
  those three have no "adaptive" thinking, the only form the app asked for.
  They are now asked to think within a token budget taken from the effort —
  1024 for `low`, 8192 for `medium` or none, 24576 for `high` and above, and
  never more than half the reply limit.
- **`grok-build-0.1` and the two `grok-4.20` models** refuse the effort setting
  itself. With any effort chosen every message to them failed; they are now
  asked again without it.
- **Titles on `gpt-6.1-sol`, Gemini 3.7 Flash and 3.8 Flash.** These refuse the
  "do not reason" a title is asked with, so chats there stayed untitled — and
  the history summary and the other background requests failed with it. They
  are asked with the lowest effort the model offers — on Gemini, the next
  level up. `youtube_watch` with one of those two Gemini models as the video
  model had the same failure.
- A Grok model named by an alias, such as `grok-4.5-latest`, has its context
  size.

## Your data

`settings.json` and the chat files move from version 4 to 5. Nothing in them is
rewritten — the step stamps the version, because `max` is kept in the settings,
in a profile's defaults and in every chat, beside each reply made under it, and
a version that does not know a value cannot read the file that holds it. 0.13.0
would have refused such settings and, worse, left a chat that held the value
out of its list without a word; stamped, it refuses the data as a newer
version's and says so. As with every schema change, the data is backed up
before it is migrated — `backups/pre-migrate-<date>.zip` — and that backup is
the way back.
